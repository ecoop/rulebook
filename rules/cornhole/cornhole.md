# Cornhole (Bags) — sample rules

A two-team outdoor game in which players take turns tossing fabric bags toward a
raised board with a hole near the top. These rules describe the common backyard
and league version for teaching and retrieval demos, not a specific league's
official rulebook.

1.1 The boards. Cornhole is played with two slanted boards, each a rectangle about
2 feet by 4 feet, raised so the far (high) end is higher than the near end. A round
hole 6 inches across is centered near the top of each board. The two boards face
each other across the court.

1.2 The bags. Each team has four bags of one colour, so eight bags are in play. A
regulation bag is a roughly 6-inch square cloth bag filled with corn or plastic
resin, heavy enough to stay where it lands but able to slide on the board.

1.3 Court and pitching distance. The two boards are placed facing each other with
their holes a set distance apart — 27 feet hole-to-hole in regulation play, less
for casual or youth games. Players throw from beside one board toward the other.

2.1 Object of the game. Each team tries to score points by landing bags on the
board or in the hole, and to reach the target score before the other team. Because
scoring cancels (see 3.3), you score both by throwing well and by covering or
knocking off the opponent's bags.

2.2 The pitch. Players alternate throwing bags one at a time. A legal pitch is
thrown underhand from behind the foul line; the thrower must release the bag below
the hip and may not cross the foul line before the bag leaves the hand.

2.3 Foul line and foul bags. The foul line is the front edge of the throwing
board. A bag thrown from past the foul line, or thrown after stepping over it, is a
foul: it scores nothing and is removed from the board before scoring, along with
any bag it knocked into a scoring position.

3.1 Bag on the board. A bag that comes to rest anywhere on the top surface of the
board, without falling off and without going through the hole, is called a "woody"
and is worth one point. A bag touching the ground and the board at the same time
does not count and is removed.

3.2 Bag in the hole. A bag that goes through the hole — whether thrown straight in
or slid in off the board or another bag — is worth three points. It does not matter
how the bag reaches the hole, only that it ends up through it.

3.3 Cancellation scoring. After all eight bags are thrown, the two teams' points
for that round cancel each other. The team with the higher raw total scores the
difference; the other team scores nothing for the round. For example, if one team
pitches 7 points and the other 4, the first team scores 3 and the second scores 0.

3.4 Winning the game. The first team to reach exactly or more than the target score
— commonly 21 points — at the end of a round wins. Standard league play requires
only reaching 21 with no need to win by two; a popular backyard variant instead
requires hitting 21 exactly and sends a team that goes over back to 15.

4.1 Innings and rotation. A round, also called an inning or a frame, is one
complete set of all eight bags thrown. The team that scored in the previous round
throws first in the next; if no one scored, the team that threw first before throws
first again.

4.2 Fouls and penalties. Besides foot faults at the foul line (2.3), a bag that
hits the ground and bounces onto the board is removed and scores nothing. Any bag
that is moved by a later foul bag is returned to its earlier position when the
players agree on where it was.
