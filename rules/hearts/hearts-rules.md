# Hearts — Rules

Hearts is a trick-taking card game for four players using a standard 52-card
deck. It is an "evasion" game: unlike most trick-taking games, the goal is to
*avoid* taking certain cards. The player with the lowest score when the game
ends is the winner.

## Players and Objective

Hearts is normally played by exactly four players, each playing individually
(there are no partnerships). The objective is to avoid taking any tricks that
contain hearts or the Queen of Spades, because those cards carry penalty
points. The game is played over multiple hands until one player reaches the
target score; at that moment the player with the *fewest* points wins.

## The Deck

A standard 52-card deck is used with no jokers. In each suit the cards rank from
highest to lowest: Ace, King, Queen, Jack, 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2. There is
no trump suit in Hearts.

## The Deal

The dealer shuffles and deals the entire deck one card at a time, clockwise, so
that each of the four players receives 13 cards. The turn to deal passes
clockwise after each hand.

## Passing Cards

Before play begins, each player selects three cards from their hand and passes
them face down to another player. Players pick the three cards they will pass
before receiving any cards from another player. The direction of the pass
rotates each hand in a four-hand cycle:

1. First hand: pass three cards to the player on your left.
2. Second hand: pass three cards to the player on your right.
3. Third hand: pass three cards to the player directly across.
4. Fourth hand: no cards are passed ("hold" or "keeper" hand).

The cycle then repeats. You must pass before you look at the cards passed to
you, and you may not pass a card you have just received in the same exchange.

## The Play

The player holding the 2 of Clubs must lead it to begin the first trick. Play
then proceeds clockwise. Each player in turn plays one card:

- You must follow suit — play a card of the suit that was led — if you have one.
- If you have no card of the suit led, you may play any other card, subject to
  the first-trick restriction below.

The trick is won by the highest card of the suit that was led. The winner of a
trick collects those four cards face down and leads any card (subject to the
"breaking hearts" rule) to the next trick.

### First-trick restriction

On the very first trick of a hand, no player may play a heart or the Queen of
Spades, even if they are void in clubs. Penalty cards cannot be discarded on the
opening trick.

### Breaking hearts

A player may not *lead* a heart until hearts have been "broken." Hearts are
broken once a player, being unable to follow suit, has discarded a heart on some
earlier trick. The only exception is when a player has nothing but hearts left
in hand, in which case a heart may be led. The Queen of Spades is not subject to
this restriction and may be led or played at any time (after the first trick).

## Scoring

After all 13 tricks have been played, players count the penalty cards they took:

- Each heart is worth 1 penalty point (13 hearts total).
- The Queen of Spades is worth 13 penalty points.

So a full hand distributes 26 penalty points. Points are recorded as a running
total across hands; a high score is bad.

## Shooting the Moon

If a single player manages to take *all* 26 penalty points in one hand — every
heart and the Queen of Spades — that player has "shot the moon." Instead of
receiving 26 points, the shooter scores 0 and each of the other three players is
charged 26 points. A common option lets the successful shooter instead subtract
26 from their own score; this should be agreed before play. A player who takes
25 of the 26 points has *not* shot the moon and simply scores those points.

## Ending the Game

The game ends at the conclusion of the hand in which any player's cumulative
score reaches or exceeds the agreed target, most commonly 100 points (50 is a
common shorter game). The player with the lowest total at that point wins. If
two or more players tie for the lowest score, additional hands are played among
the tied players, or the tie is broken by prior agreement.

## Common Variants

- **Jack of Diamonds (Omnibus Hearts):** the Jack of Diamonds (sometimes the
  10 of Diamonds) is worth −10 points, rewarding the player who takes it.
- **No passing every hand:** some groups drop the "hold" hand or change the
  rotation order.
- **Different player counts:** Hearts can be adapted for three, five, or six
  players by removing low cards so the deck divides evenly; passing and the
  opening-lead card adjust accordingly.
- **Target score:** 50 or 100 are both common; agree before starting.
